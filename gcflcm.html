Chapter 2

What are GCF & LCM?

GCF — Greatest Common Factor
FPB — Faktor Persekutuan Terbesar

The largest number that divides two or more numbers exactly (with no remainder).

Example: GCF of 12 and 18
Factors of 12: 1, 2, 3, 6, 12
Factors of 18: 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18
Common factors: 1, 2, 3, 6 → GCF = 6
LCM — Least Common Multiple
KPK — Kelipatan Persekutuan Terkecil

The smallest number that is a multiple of two or more numbers.

Example: LCM of 4 and 6
Multiples of 4: 4, 8, 12, 16, 20...
Multiples of 6: 6, 12, 18, 24...
Common multiples: 12, 24, 36... → LCM = 12

Common Factor

A number that is a factor of two or more numbers. Example: 3 is a common factor of 12 and 18 because 12 ÷ 3 = 4 and 18 ÷ 3 = 6 (both divide exactly).

Common Multiple

A number that is a multiple of two or more numbers. Example: 12 is a common multiple of 4 and 6 because 4 × 3 = 12 and 6 × 2 = 12.

Real-Life Uses

When to use GCF
  • Splitting items into equal groups or gift bags
  • Finding the largest tile to cover a floor
  • Dividing classes into equal teams
When to use LCM
  • Two buses arriving at different intervals
  • Bells ringing at different times
  • Finding the smallest package size

Chapter 4

Prime Factors & Factor Trees

A prime number has only two factors: 1 and itself. Every composite number can be broken down into prime factors using a factor tree.

How to Build a Factor Tree

  1. Write the number at the top.
  2. Find two factors of the number.
  3. Branch them out below.
  4. For each composite factor, branch again.
  5. Stop when all leaves are prime.
  6. Circle the prime numbers — they are your answer!

Writing the Answer

For 24: 2 × 2 × 2 × 3
Multiplication form: 2 × 2 × 2 × 3
Exponent form: 2³ × 3
Type exponents using ^ (e.g. 2^3 becomes 2³). Use the × button for multiplication.
Chapter 5

LCM & GCF Using Prime Factorisation

Use prime factorisation to find GCF and LCM of 2 or 3 numbers efficiently.

GCF Method

Take the lowest power of each common prime factor, then multiply.

12 = 2² × 3¹
18 = 2¹ × 3²
GCF = 2¹ × 3¹ = 6

LCM Method

Take the highest power of each prime factor (from any number), then multiply.

12 = 2² × 3¹
18 = 2¹ × 3²
LCM = 2² × 3² = 36

Key Takeaways

  • GCF = largest common factor (use for splitting/grouping)
  • LCM = smallest common multiple (use for repeating events)
  • Every composite number has a unique prime factorisation
  • Factor trees help us find prime factors visually
  • GCF: take lowest powers of common primes
  • LCM: take highest powers of all primes
